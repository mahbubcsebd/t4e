From idea to real product: how YogiFi designed and built its Smart Yoga teacher portal

YogiFi makes what it calls the world’s first smart yoga mat that detects your posture and gives real-time feedback while you practice. Built-in pressure sensors sync with the YogiFi app for AI-guided sessions and posture correction. With a new idea and limited resources, a Smart Yoga teacher portal had always been on the back burner. That’s when YogiFi founder Muralidhar Somisetty used Think4Ever to design the portal end to end before coding and testing it, and to carry it from requirements through to deployment.

The challenge

Every founder with a new idea faces the same problem: the only way to find out whether the idea works is to put a real product in front of real users, and building that product usually takes a team the company does not have yet. For YogiFi, that meant:

  • A new idea that has to prove itself in the field. A smart yoga mat only shows its value when people practice on it. YogiFi needed working software quickly to validate its real impact, not just a prototype or a pitch.
  • Limited resources. An early-stage startup cannot staff separate product management, design and engineering functions, yet the product still needs all three.
  • Getting the design right before writing code. The teacher portal extends YogiFi from home practice to yoga teachers, who can remotely monitor their students’ practice on the smart mat and give personalized 1:1 coaching based on the insights from the mat. Mistakes in requirements, flows or the data model are expensive to undo once code is written.
  • Code alone is not a product. Generative AI coding tools can produce code quickly, but they skip the requirements, design and data model a growing product depends on, and leave little for the rest of the organization to work from.

The solution: design first, then build

Instead of prompting an AI coding tool one change at a time, the founder captured the idea once and let Think4Ever generate the artifacts of the full software development lifecycle from that shared context. Each step builds on an approved earlier one instead of being reinvented by hand.

StepWhat Think4Ever producedHow it helped
1. RequirementsObjectives and business rulesThe idea for the teacher portal is captured as objectives, business rules and acceptance criteria, then reviewed and approved in the Living Blueprint.
2. DesignArchitecture, flows and UIThink4Ever generates the architecture, business and user flows, and UI designs, so the portal can be reviewed and refined before any code is written.
3. DataDatabase schemaThink4Ever designs the data objects and database schema behind the portal, consistent with the approved requirements.
4. CodeWorking applicationCoding agents build the portal from the approved design and business rules rather than from isolated prompts.
5. Test and deployTested releaseThe portal is tested and deployed, and the Blueprint stays current as YogiFi learns from teachers in the field.

Value for everyone on the team

Because each artifact is generated and kept current, the work is useful well beyond engineering. As YogiFi grows its team, each role starts from something concrete.

Product managers

Approved objectives, business rules and acceptance criteria that define what the portal must do.

Product designers

User flows and UI designs that can be reviewed and changed before development starts.

Engineers

Architecture, database schema and working code built from the approved design, plus a clear view of how a change affects the rest of the system.

Validating impact faster

With a working, deployed portal, YogiFi can put its product in front of teachers and students and learn from real use. Feedback flows back into the Blueprint, and the next version is built from the updated design.

Why this matters for founders

  • From idea to real product. A founder can turn a new idea into deployed software and start validating it in the field without first building a full team.
  • Design before code. Requirements, flows and the data model are reviewed before development starts, which avoids expensive rework later.
